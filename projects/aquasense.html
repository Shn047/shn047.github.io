Who it's for

Small and medium fish farms in Southeast Asia that can't afford real-time monitoring.

What we designed

A solar-powered sensor and camera unit feeding a cloud analytics pipeline and a mobile alert dashboard.

Result

Innovative Business Idea Award and a projected ฿330k a year in savings for a 20-hectare farm.

The problem

Fish farmers are working blind.

Small and medium fish farms in Southeast Asia lack affordable, real-time monitoring. Problems are only noticed when it's already too late.

High mortality

Problems are spotted after fish die.

Farmers react to water-quality issues only once fish start dying.

Feed waste

"Blind" feeding costs money.

Without knowing fish size, farms overfeed, raising costs and polluting the water.

Turbid water

Existing cameras can't see.

Camera solutions fail in the muddy, cloudy water typical of Asian aquaculture.

System design

From sensors in the pond to an alert on the phone.

AquaSense is designed as an end-to-end IoT analytics system. Underwater sensors and cameras collect data, a base station sends it to the cloud for analysis, and the farmer sees clear indicators and alerts in a mobile app.

CollectSensors + cameraspH · DO · ammonia · tempSubmersible chemical sensors track pH, dissolved oxygen, ammonia and temperature; stereoscopic cameras capture depth images of the fish.
TransmitBase stationSolar-powered, off-gridIntegrated solar panels let the unit run off-grid on remote farms.
AnalyseCloud + deep learningTrends · biomassAggregates readings, detects worsening trends, and uses computer vision to estimate fish size and biomass.
ActMobile dashboardAlerts · feed adviceShows water status, sends alerts before conditions turn critical, and supports feed optimisation.

The data angle

Decision support, not just tracking.

Collecting data is the easy part. The value comes from turning raw sensor readings into simple indicators, trends and alerts that a farmer can act on quickly, warning them before water quality becomes critical and reducing both fish deaths and energy use.

Business model (concept)

Product-as-a-Service: farms subscribe to the analytics instead of buying expensive equipment up front, which lowers the barrier for smaller farms.

What I learned

  • Usability beats data volume. An analytics system is only useful if the people using it can act on what it shows.
  • Dashboards turn data into action. The design of the indicators and alerts matters as much as the sensors.
  • Context makes the case. Combining data, system design and business context is what made the idea convincing to judges.
