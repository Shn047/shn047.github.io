Who it's for

Absolute beginners preparing for HSK 1: 307 words across 15 lessons.

What we built

A Flutter app (Android and web) on Supabase, plus a FastAPI server on AWS EC2 running three AI services.

Result

Live beta at hanziflow.site. The AI checks are strict by design: they never accepted a wrong answer in our tests.

The problem

Beginner apps reward guessing, not learning.

Chinese is hard to start: four tones change a word's meaning, and characters must be written stroke by stroke. Most apps help learners memorise, but rarely tell them honestly when they are wrong.

Pronunciation

Speech recognition hides tone errors.

Standard speech-to-text "auto-corrects" to the closest real word, so a wrong tone is often accepted.

Writing

Handwriting is rarely checked.

Learners trace characters, but few apps verify whether what they wrote is actually right.

Vocabulary

Words are learned without context.

Memorised word lists don't turn into words learners can actually use in a sentence.

Integration

Skills are learned separately.

Pronunciation, writing and sentence structure live in different tools with no single learning flow.

What the app does

Five features, one learning flow.

  1. 01 · Pinyin Playground

    Build syllables, hear all 4 tones

    Combine initials and finals on an interactive grid and compare tones side by side (mā vs mǎ).

  2. 02 · Word Cards

    307 HSK 1 words

    Each card pairs the character with an image, native audio, an example sentence and its grammar pattern.

  3. 03 · Character Writing

    Watch, trace, then get checked

    Stroke animation, ghost tracing, then an ML check that says correct, incomplete or wrong character.

  4. 04 · Pronunciation

    Tone-aware, syllable-level scores

    Record a word and get a score per syllable, judged on the tone actually spoken, without auto-correction.

  5. 05 · Context Generation

    Your words, in a real sentence

    Pick 2–3 learned words and get a short story that uses only HSK 1 vocabulary and grammar.

  6. + Quizzes

    HSK 1 exam practice

    Eight types of listening and reading questions in the official exam format, lesson checkpoints, and a full mock exam on the official HSK 1 scoring scale.

Progress & motivation

XP, a daily streak and badges reward regular practice. The profile also turns the learner's mastered words into an HSK 1 progress bar and an estimate of the study time left, at about 15 minutes per word.

Architecture

A Flutter app, one API server, three AI services.

The Flutter app talks to Supabase for sign-in, learner progress and audio files, and to a single FastAPI server on AWS EC2 (behind Nginx) for anything that needs AI. All three AI services run in that one process.

How the AI works

Three services, each built to be honest.

1 · Pronunciation: score the tone that was spoken

A Wav2Vec2 (XLSR-53) model with separate CTC heads for initials, finals and tones listens to the recording. The target pinyin is known in advance (e.g. ni3 hao3), so for each syllable the system finds the audio frames where the target is most likely and compares it with the strongest competing token.

If hao4 beats the expected hao3, the learner is told exactly that, instead of the word being silently accepted. Scores are grouped as Good (≥ 60%), Needs practice (30–60%) or Incorrect (< 30%). The app also applies tone-change rules: for 你好 it tells the learner to say ní hǎo, the way it is really spoken.

2 · Handwriting: check the character, then the strokes

The drawing is cropped, centred and turned into a 40 × 40 greyscale image. A ResNet-style CNN converts it into a 512-dimension embedding, which is compared with templates for 256 HSK 1 characters using cosine similarity. Above 90% similarity it's correct, below that it's incomplete, and if another character matches clearly better it's flagged as the wrong character. A stroke checker then looks at order and joins, so feedback is specific: "write #2 撇 before #3 横", with the rule behind it ("this 横 comes after the stroke it crosses, same in 女、子").

Recording shown at 1.5× speed. The app itself notes that writing scores are still being calibrated.

3 · Context generation: the LLM writes, the backend decides

DeepSeek (through OpenRouter) writes a short story from the learner's chosen words. Nothing it says about its own output is trusted: the backend re-checks every sentence itself and builds the pinyin from the app's own dictionary.

Validation layer

  • Every word is segmented and checked against the 307-word HSK 1 list
  • Length and grammar must fit the approved HSK 1 patterns
  • Up to 3 retries, then a template fallback; if nothing passes, the app shows an error rather than bad Chinese

Evaluation

Strict on purpose: never accept a wrong answer.

For beginners, praising a mistake is worse than asking for a retry, because it locks in a bad habit. Both models were tuned to be strict, and the tests show exactly that trade-off.

Next: user testing

  • 5–10 absolute beginners use word cards, pronunciation, writing, context generation and quizzes.
  • Pronunciation: compare app scores with a Chinese teacher's ratings (Pearson correlation).
  • Writing: character accuracy against a teacher's judgement.
  • Usability: SUS score and 1–5 satisfaction ratings.

What I took away

  • Choose the right error to avoid. For learners, a false "correct" costs more than an extra retry.
  • Never trust the LLM's word. A deterministic validation layer is what makes generated content safe to show.
  • The app makes the model useful. Recording, uploads, waiting states and clear per-syllable feedback decide whether learners trust an AI score.
