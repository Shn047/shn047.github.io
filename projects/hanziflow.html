Who it's for

Chinese learners who want to read real websites, not just textbook sentences.

What we built

A QLoRA fine-tuned Qwen3-8B model plus a grammar RAG pipeline, served through FastAPI to a Firefox extension.

Result

Comparable to Jieba in a side-by-side review, with no paid API, running in a working extension.

The challenge

Two problems: one for learners, one for the business.

Problem 1 · For learners

Chinese has no spaces, so beginners don't know where words end.

In English, spaces show where each word starts and stops. Chinese runs together, so a beginner has to guess which characters belong together before they can look anything up, and a wrong split gives the wrong meaning.

Problem 2 · For the business

Segmentation through third-party APIs is costly and hard to scale.

Many NLP applications rely on third-party APIs for text processing. For high-volume workloads that brings:

  • High cost that grows with every request
  • Latency from calling an external service
  • Limited customisation for domain-specific text
  • Scaling limits outside your own control

Free tools like Jieba avoid the cost but often mis-split domain-specific text.

The goal

Help learners read real Chinese websites, with a segmentation model that is accurate, cheap to run and practical to deploy, instead of paying for an external API.

Approach

Small model, fine-tuned for one job.

Instead of calling a large general-purpose model, we picked the best small open model for the job and fine-tuned it with QLoRA, a parameter-efficient method that trains small adapter weights on top of a 4-bit quantised model, keeping compute costs low.

  1. 01

    Prepare data

    Cleaned Chinese Treebank 8.0 from 71,369 to 17,637 segmentation sentences, and scraped 434 grammar patterns (A1–B2) from the Chinese Grammar Wiki.

  2. 02

    Compare 4 models

    Fine-tuned Qwen3-8B, DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and IBM Granite 4.0 on a subset, judged by human experts proficient in Chinese, alongside Jieba and ChatGPT.

  3. 03

    Fine-tune the winner

    Instruction-tuned Qwen3-8B (4-bit) with QLoRA in Unsloth for 2 epochs, published on Hugging Face as Qwen3_segmented ↗.

System architecture

From a text selection to a word-by-word breakdown.

I co-designed the system that connects the fine-tuned model to the browser. The extension sends the selected text to a FastAPI service on a remote VM, where the inference system handles two jobs: the fine-tuned model segments the sentence, and a Haystack RAG pipeline finds matching grammar structures.

Evaluation

Checked side by side against Jieba and ChatGPT.

To check the fine-tuned model, we ran the same sentences through it, Jieba (the most widely used open-source Chinese segmentation tool) and ChatGPT, and reviewed the results by hand.

What we compared

  • Segmentation quality and consistency on task-relevant text
  • Practical usefulness for downstream tasks such as word lookups and grammar patterns

Outcome

In this human-reviewed comparison, the fine-tuned model's output was comparable to Jieba's, without depending on external NLP APIs. It was a qualitative review, not a scored benchmark; measuring F1 on a held-out test set is the next step.

Interface

Learning happens on the page you're already reading.

Selecting text opens an overlay with each word, its English meaning, and the sentence's usage pattern, so learners never leave the article.

Impact

Business and engineering

  • Reduced dependency on costly third-party NLP APIs.
  • Showed that small language models are viable for cost-sensitive applications.
  • Balanced performance, cost and scalability in one system design.

What I learned

Takeaways

  • Parameter-efficient fine-tuning makes deploying language models practical.
  • Comparing against an established tool is what makes an AI business case credible.
  • System integration matters as much as model accuracy in real products.
